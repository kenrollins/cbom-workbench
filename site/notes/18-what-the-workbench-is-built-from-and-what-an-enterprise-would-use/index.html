    What is the workbench built from, why each part, and what would an enterprise run in its place?¶

    The answer¶

    The workbench is assembled from standards and open source tools, one per job, each chosen because its output can be checked by something other than itself: a scanner's finding by a second scanner and a probe, a model's answer by a rule or a re-scan, a ledger entry by a hash chain and a signature. Every part has an enterprise equivalent. Swapping one in changes scale, support and who signs the audit, not the method: the same CBOM, the same ledger, the same paired proof on the wire.

    The concept¶

    A cryptography bill of materials (CBOM) is an inventory of the algorithms, keys, certificates and protocols an estate uses, with where each was found. Building one takes discovery (reading the wire, the host and the code), a record (where findings go and how they are kept honest), a document (the CBOM a consumer reads), judgement (which findings matter, by policy), change (what to do and in what order) and proof (that a change worked). The workbench has one component per step. The table below names each, the reason it was chosen, and what an enterprise would run instead.

    The mechanism, on one box¶

    On 2026-11-04 the whole table ran on one DGX Spark (GB10, aarch64, 128 GB unified memory): the bundle loaded in 8 s, the mini-agency came up, a live scan finished in under five minutes with the same wire score as the lab's x86_64 run (35 of 40, no false positives), and three engines sat beside it: Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B-A3B under vLLM for the fast tier (a Nemotron Nano 9B v2 NIM held the slot first and reached no role's floor), Nemotron 3.5 Super VL 120B-A12B under vLLM for the deliberate tier and ollama's mxbai-embed-large for the embed tier. Which engine serves which role is decided by the spike's floors on that box, not by the table above; lesson 49 records what an engine decides for itself before any role can reach it.

    What follows¶

    An agency choosing between this method and a commercial platform is not choosing a different method. The discovery evidence, the CBOM standard and the paired proof are the same; what the platform adds is scale (thousands of hosts, scheduled), support, and a vendor's name on the audit. What the workbench adds is that every step can be checked by a reader with the repository: the rule beside its source, the model's score beside its floor, the ledger beside its signature. The two are complementary: the workbench's CBOM can be uploaded to a platform and judged there (lesson 48), and a platform's CBOM can be imported here and scored against the answer key.

    Scope¶

    This note names the parts as they stand in the repository on 2026-11-04 and what ran on one machine that day. It does not compare the commercial platforms with each other, and it names no measurement of one. Enterprise equivalents are categories with examples, not recommendations.
