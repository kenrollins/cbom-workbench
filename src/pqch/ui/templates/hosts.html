A network scan sees a server as the listeners it exposes. Much of what decides its future sits below that: the keys its firmware trusts to boot, a management controller on the same board with its own TLS, SSH and certificates, and key files on its disk. Each moves on its own clock — a setting, an operating-system update, a firmware update, or the vendor. A host's plan says which is which. Every row is the host's latest system scan. A recording is a scan made elsewhere, its identifiers replaced, replayed here into a new signed ledger; the times shown are when the host was read.
