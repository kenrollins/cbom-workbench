1 The estate, every handshake

Inside the ring: each VLAN an arc, each device a point on it, listeners inside the house marked. Outside: the names the house talks to. Every line is a client–server pair, bundled, coloured by what the pair negotiated. Hover a point to isolate it; click to hold.

How to read it

Green lines negotiated a hybrid post-quantum group. Red lines are the ones to look at: the client offered a hybrid group and the server chose a classical one. Grey lines never had a hybrid group on offer. A listener inside the house fed by many red lines is enabled nowhere and could be with one setting; a device with only grey lines cannot be fixed from the server side at all. Line width is the number of handshakes.

2 The house against the internet

Per hour: the share of handshakes that negotiated a hybrid group, inside the house and to the outside, and the share where a client offered one. Below it, each VLAN on its own.

What the gap between the two lines means

Where "offered" sits above "negotiated", clients asked for post-quantum key exchange and servers declined. Outside the house that gap is the providers that have not moved; inside, it is the listeners in the table below. Cloudflare Radar publishes the same share for the Internet at large — about 70% of browser traffic to its network by its own figure — which is the bar to read these lines against.

3 Who can offer what

Every client inside the house, grouped by VLAN, against every key-exchange group seen on offer, strongest first. A dot: the client offered it. A filled dot: it was also the client's first choice. The last columns are the TLS versions it offered and what the rule concluded.

What the rows at the bottom of each block are

A device that offers no hybrid group but does offer TLS 1.3 is current enough to be fixed by a software update; a device with no TLS 1.3 at all will never negotiate ML-KEM, because hybrid key exchange is a TLS 1.3 mechanism. Those rows are the ones a migration plan has to replace, not configure. The rule is capability/observed/1; its event is cited on each row.
